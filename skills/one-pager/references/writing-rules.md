## 1. Facts only

- Every prospect-specific statement traces to a ledger row with status `VERIFIED` or `STATED`. If you cannot point at the row, cut the sentence.
- Quote the prospect's numbers as they appear. Computed figures (differences, counts, averages) are allowed when the arithmetic is in the notes; label them plainly ("average gap on those nights").
- "About", "roughly" and "around" are allowed on a prospect figure only when the figure is computed and the notes show the arithmetic. Time estimates in the effort line and the ask ("about an hour", "20 minutes") are not prospect figures and may carry them.
- No adjectives about the prospect's business (beautiful, impressive, well-run, popular) unless they quote a sourced review or the prospect's own words, with attribution on the page.
- `BENCHMARK` figures appear at most once, labelled as an industry figure with the source name, and never inside the figure blocks.
- `INFERRED` statements appear only as a question in the ask or as an "if" in what it would take ("if the rate feed is the cause, the fix is one rule").
- `UNSOURCED` facts never appear, and the page never says that something could not be found or checked.
- Sender-side claims (results, clients, years in business) follow the same rule: a ledger row with a source, or nothing. The page is about the prospect; the sender appears in the footer and in the effort line.

## 2. Never invent rapport

- No warm-up lines. Banned openers: "I hope this finds you well", "hope you're well", "I loved your", "big fan", "congrats on", "I've been following", "came across your", "it's been a while", "mutual friend".
- A sourced connection (met at a named event on a date, introduced by a named person who agreed to be named, a call on a date) may appear in email line 1, stated as the fact it is, and never on the page.
- No compliments. No shared-interest lines. No "as a fellow".

## 3. No em dashes

- U+2014 never appears in any of the three files. U+2013 never appears as a pause. Ranges use "to": "15 to 18%", "June to September".
- Rewrite with a comma, a colon, a full stop, or parentheses. Two short sentences usually beat one joined sentence.
- Check with the commands in section 11.

## 4. No confession copy

- The prospect never reads about the sender's process, rework, doubts, or misses. Banned: "we went back", "on a second look", "we initially", "we corrected", "we revised", "we weren't able to", "we couldn't find", "we're not sure", "I think", "I believe", "probably", "apologies", "sorry", "to be honest", "honestly", "full disclosure", "we missed", "turns out", "after digging".
- Present the finished state and the method. Rigour is shown by stating the standard once, in the footer ("Every figure on this page comes from ... checked on <date>"), never by narrating the checking.
- This rule is about the sender's own work. Disclosing a risk in the prospect's decision is allowed and welcome ("if the rate rule is wrong, it reverses in a day").

## 5. No first/founding language

- Never position the prospect as the first, a founding client, a pilot, a beta, an early adopter, a charter member, a design partner, a case study, or as "helping us shape" anything.
- Never position the sender as new, founding, just starting, or "building". Never mention how long the sender has existed. If the sender's title is founder, the footer carries the firm name alone; the title adds nothing the page needs.
- Grep targets: first, founding, founder, pilot, beta, early, charter, inaugural, launch, new, case study. Keep a hit only where it describes the prospect's own facts or an ordinal step ("the first step is").

## 6. Numbers on their own line

- On the page, every prospect figure sits in a figure block: the number on one line, its label beneath. Three blocks maximum.
- Body paragraphs may refer to a figure that is already in a block. A new prospect figure never appears for the first time inside a body paragraph. If a fourth figure matters, it replaces one of the three.
- A step in "what it would take" may carry one figure, because each step is its own line. The one benchmark figure, if used, sits in a one-sentence paragraph of its own.
- In the email, at most one figure, and the line that carries it carries only that sentence.
- Never trail a stat after a comma. "Nineteen nights above the OTA, a $16 gap" is banned. Write the figure, full stop, then the next sentence.
- Write figures as the prospect reads them: "$16", "19 of 30 nights", "64 rooms". Percentages only where the base is shown or obvious.
- Dates and time units anywhere on the page ("20 minutes", "this week", "the end of 2026") do not count as figures for this rule.

## 7. Speech is written as speech

- Any line meant to be said aloud (a suggested opening line for the call, a voicemail, a talk track in the notes) is written the way it would be said: contractions, one idea per sentence, no parenthetical asides, no colons, no lists, no decimals.
- Read it aloud once. If you would not say it that way to the person, rewrite it.
- The email is read, not spoken, but the same test catches stiffness: if a line would sound odd said across a table, shorten it.

## 8. Tone

- The finding is a fact and the door it opens. "Your own site is priced above the OTA on 19 of 30 nights" is a finding. "Your pricing is broken" is a verdict. Never a verdict.
- No urgency the facts do not carry: no "before the season", "this week only", "limited".
- No hype adjectives and no corporate vocabulary: leverage, synergy, circle back, low-hanging fruit, game-changing, best-in-class, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, unlock, supercharge, elevate, empower, world-class, revolutionary, transform, journey, solution, reach out, touch base.
- Plain words. Short sentences. Contractions if the voice profile uses them.
- The ask is a question, never a command and never a menu.

## 9. Length budgets

| Element | Budget |
|---|---|
| Page prose: the paragraphs, steps and ask inside the four cards (what command 4 counts) | 380 words |
| Where they stand | 90 words plus up to 3 figure blocks |
| One finding | 120 words, two paragraphs, plus one optional benchmark sentence |
| What it would take | 3 steps of 25 words each, plus one effort line of 30 words |
| One ask | 40 words, one question |
| Footer | sender line, standard line, sources line |
| Email subject | 6 words |
| Email body | 3 lines, 70 words (whole file with subject, greeting and sign-off: 80) |
| Printed | one sheet, A4 or Letter |

## 10. Banned phrases

Rapport: hope this finds you, hope you're well, loved your, big fan, congrats, been following, came across, mutual friend, as a fellow.
Corporate: reach out, touch base, circle back, leverage, synergy, low-hanging fruit, game-changing, best-in-class, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, unlock, supercharge, elevate, empower, world-class, revolutionary, transform, journey, solution, excited, thrilled.
Confession: honestly, to be honest, full disclosure, apologies, sorry, turns out, after digging, we initially, we missed, we went back, we corrected, we revised, we couldn't, we weren't, not sure, I think, I believe, probably.
Founding: founding, founder, pilot, beta, early adopter, charter, inaugural, design partner, case study.
Urgency: this week only, limited time, before the season.

Add the sender's own never-use words from the voice profile to the grep before running it.

## 11. Self-review checklist and commands

- [ ] Every prospect statement on the page maps to a `VERIFIED` or `STATED` ledger row; the notes list the row numbers beside each paragraph
- [ ] Figure blocks: three at most, none introduced mid-paragraph, email carries at most one
- [ ] One finding, stated as fact plus the door it opens, no verdict
- [ ] One ask, one question, with a time unit
- [ ] Email: three lines, greeting and sign-off verbatim from the profile, no preamble, no introduction of the sender
- [ ] Zero dashes, zero banned phrases; hand-review hits reviewed one by one
- [ ] Voice: longest sentence within the profile's longest; no never-use words
- [ ] Word counts within budget; prints on one sheet
- [ ] Notes list every `INFERRED` and `UNSOURCED` row under "Not on the page"

Run from the output folder, with `<slug>` filled in:

```bash
P=<slug>-page.html; E=<slug>-email.txt; N=<slug>-notes.md
sed '/<style>/,/<\/style>/d' $P > page-body.tmp   # the page without its stylesheet, so CSS words never trip a grep

# 1. Dashes, all three files. Byte form, so it works in any locale, on GNU grep and on macOS
LC_ALL=C grep -nE $'\xe2\x80\x94|\xe2\x80\x93' $P $E $N

# 2. Banned phrases, page body and email. Append the sender's never-use words with |word
grep -niE 'hope (this|you)|big fan|loved your|congrats|been following|came across|mutual friend|as a fellow|reach(ing)? out|touch(ing)? base|circle back|leverag|synerg|low.hanging|game.chang|best.in.class|seamless|cutting.edge|supercharge|world.class|revolution|\b(robust|unlock|elevate|empower|transform|journey|solution|excited|thrilled|honestly)\b|to be honest|full disclosure|apolog|sorry|turns out|after digging|we (initially|missed|went back|corrected|revised|couldn|weren|are not sure|re not sure)|not sure|i think|i believe|probably|founding|founder|\b(pilot|beta|charter|inaugural)\b|early adopter|design partner|case study|this week only|limited time|before the season' page-body.tmp $E

# 3. Hand-review words: keep a hit only for the prospect's own facts or an ordinal step
grep -niE '\b(first|early|launch|new)\b' page-body.tmp $E

# 4. Word counts: page prose (paragraphs, steps, ask; keep inline tags out of them) against 380, email file against 80
grep -oE '<(p|p class="bench"|li|div class="ask")>[^<]*' page-body.tmp | sed 's/<[^>]*>//' | wc -w
wc -w $E
rm page-body.tmp
```

Commands 1 and 2 must print nothing. Command 3 prints lines to read by hand. Command 4 must be at or under the budgets in section 9. The whole page with title, figure labels and footer lands near 450 words at that budget; the one-sheet print is the final check.
