Written for a seller whose service produces conversations or deals for the prospect
(outbound, appointment setting, lead generation, partnerships). Adapt nouns for other
services; keep the shape.

---

## 1. How to use this bank

Pick, do not paste. A SKELETON carries six to fifteen discovery questions, chosen because
the last call left them open. Every question you take must be rewritten so that it stands
alone against the BRIEFED block: swap "your live deal" for the briefed name of that deal,
swap "your partner" for the briefed name. Keep each as one spoken sentence.

The one question in bold under B is the minute-12 number for most deals. If the prospect
holds a different decisive figure, promote that one instead.

## 2. VERIFY (before the pitch)

Ask these before showing anything or quoting anything. They take two minutes and stop
you pitching the wrong deal.

Goal and alternate shapes
- New <contracts | accounts | clients> are the goal, is that right?
- Would you also take a <consulting-only | referral | subcontract | pilot> shape, or is
  it the full <contract> or nothing?
- If the buyer already has an outside company doing this, is that a prospect or a dead end?

Geography
- <The footprint from the notes>, is that right? Anything you'd add or drop?
- Would you go to <the place they hinted at>?
- What has to be true for a far-away one to be worth it: they pay travel, a minimum size?

Who decides
- Is this your call, or does <briefed name> need to be in?
- Who else has to say yes on your side before you sign anything?

ICP edges
- <Segment they do not chase today>: same deal in a different building, or not your
  business?
- <Boundary case from the notes>: does that count for you, or is it a dead end?
- Whatever they pick: "then that's a second list, it goes on the next page." Note it;
  do not build it in the room.

## 3. Discovery A. Who to hunt

- Where do new <deals> come from today? What happens when that runs dry?
- Describe the <buyer> you'd most want to sign. And the one you'd walk away from.
- Who do you already know in <the footprint>? Anyone mid-conversation now we must not touch?
- What is an instant no for you?

## 4. Discovery B. Economics (cannot leave without)

- On <their live deal, briefed by name>: is it a flat fee, a percentage, equity, something
  else?
- **What does a typical <deal> make you in year one, after costs?**
- Base, incentive on top, anything up front?
- What does your best <deal> pay you in year one, and what does a typical one pay?
  (If they are all similar, one flat fee is right for you. If they spread widely, tier.)
- How long from signing to the <deal> actually paying you?
- Is there a fee before <the doors open | go-live>, and does the base run from day one or
  only once it is profitable?
- How many new <deals> could you take on in twelve months without breaking?
- First real conversation to signature: how long, and how many convert?
- On the buyer's side, who has to say yes: the owner, whoever runs it day to day, a board,
  procurement?
- How did your last one find you?

## 5. Discovery C. Cash

- When a new <deal> comes on, what does it cost you before it starts paying you?
- Does a fee at signing sit awkwardly against that, or does the timing work?
- If they flinch, offer shape not discount: more monthly and less on the win, or less
  monthly and more on the win?

## 6. Discovery D. What we book

- Describe the first conversation you'd thank us for.
- Who has to be in the seat? Who is a waste of your hour?
- How many first calls a week can you actually take, and who takes them?
- What do you need to know about a buyer before you'll take the call?

## 7. Discovery E. If time (running it)

- Whose name does the outreach go out under, and whose calendar do calls land on?
- A handful of real emails you've sent to <buyers>, so we can match your voice?
- Who reviews drafts, and how fast?
- Any names we must never contact?

## 8. Answers to have ready

Questions prospects ask sellers of this kind of service. Prepare the answer in the
CALL NOTES under WHAT THEY ASKED US if it was asked, otherwise in your head. Each answer
follows Rule 6: finished state and method, never the rework.

- How did you build this? (The sources cross-referenced and the standard each row met.)
- How accurate is it? (What each row was checked against. Where a record could not settle
  it, the page says so. That is a risk disclosure, which is allowed.)
- Do you just hand us a list? (What the list is versus what the work is.)
- How fast? (No date. Say what has to happen first and why.)
- Have you done this in our industry? (Say what you have done, with numbers you can
  defend. Never "first", never "founding".)
- Why a retainer if you get paid on the close? (What the retainer covers, in one sentence.)
- We know a lot of these people. (Good; they go on a protected list day one.)
- I'm not sure we can afford it. (Do not argue it. Move to the terms that make the
  asymmetry obvious and let them do the arithmetic aloud.)
