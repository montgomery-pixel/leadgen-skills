The 10-minute walkthrough

One run of each skill, start to finish.

Three Claude Code skills that do the first steps of B2B lead generation the way a research-first outreach shop does it. Map a whole market from public records. Turn a sales call into notes you can prove. Send one page per prospect built from their own numbers.

This page shows one run of each skill, in pack order: the command you type, what the skill does at each of its numbered steps, and the files it writes, shown as they come out. Every example is fictional (Harbor Lights Hotel, Fensworth Clubs, Fairway and Fern, Dana Reyes at Fensworth Advisory). None of it is a real client.

  • prospect-map

    In: a niche and a geography.

    Out: market.csv (the count), rows.json (owner, status, evidence per entity), map.html (the page with a dated watch list).

  • discovery-sheet

    In: a call transcript or your notes.

    Out: CALL-NOTES-<prospect>-<date>.md (facts, quotes, every number on its own line, the ICP in their words) and SKELETON-<prospect>-<next>.md (what you hold on the next call).

  • one-pager

    In: a research file with sourced facts, optionally your voice profile.

    Out: <prospect>-page.html (print-ready), <prospect>-email.txt (three lines plus your sign-off), <prospect>-notes.md (a source per claim).

They chain. rows.json from the map is a valid research file for one-pager. So is the CALL-NOTES file from discovery-sheet.

01

prospect-map

Count every business in your niche and geography from public records. Every row carries its owner, its status and the evidence behind it, dated.

The command

Form: /prospect-map "<niche>" "<geography>". This run:

/prospect-map "restaurant operator for full-service hotels" "North Shore coast"

The first argument is what you sell and to whom, the second is where to count. The skill writes its working folder at prospect-map/<niche>-<geography>/, pulls the public licence and parcel files first, and runs verification agents only on the rows that need them. This market covers three regions: North Shore, East Valley and Cape Bell.

What it does

  1. The split question. Before touching a source, write into journal.md who pays whom, the one question a public record can answer, the four tests for this niche, the inclusion rule for the count and the region granularity.
  2. Enumerate in bulk. Pull the state licence file, county parcel layers and association lists, filter to the inclusion rule, dedupe on an address key, write market.csv, run the address join, then seed rows.json with one row per entity.
  3. Owner of record. Bulk parcel pull, join by address, cluster by owner mailing address, unmask the LLC through the registry and filings. Write owner, ownerConfidence and ownerEvidence; unknown says unknown with the reason.
  4. Verify in refute mode. One agent per candidate row tries to disprove it and may keep it only with evidence dated this year. Four tests (separate party, employer of record, inside the premises, live today) decide ready, licensed, verify, transition or aside.
  5. Watch-list triggers. Attach a dated, sourced trigger to any row where the record shows a sale, an ownership or operator change, a renovation or closure, a new build, an authority-held title or a default. Fact, not forecast.
  6. Render. Rewrite map.config.json for this market, run render_map.py --check, then render map.html. The renderer computes every number on the page from the rows and refuses blank evidence, a missing date, an unknown status or a dash in any rendered field.
  7. Honesty pass. Open map.html and check: every row states its evidence and a date, no contacts, no personal names, no narration of rework, the Ready count described as a floor, and five rows re-opened at random against their cited source.

What comes out

Three files. market.csv holds the count, one line per hotel. rows.json holds every entity with its owner, counterparty, status, evidence and trigger. map.html is the page, and its header on this market reads:

02

discovery-sheet

Turn a call transcript into notes you can prove: every number, every quote, the ICP in their words, plus the next-call sheet.

The input

A 24-minute speaker-labelled transcript. Sam Ortega sells an outbound service that books first conversations with buyers. Priya Lindqvist is CEO of Fairway and Fern, which runs outsourced grounds maintenance for private golf clubs. Sam sent a one-page proposal before the call. Two lines from the file:

calls/fairway-fern-2026-03-10.txtexcerpt
[01:05] SAM: What does a club pay you?
[01:09] PRIYA: Flat monthly. Somewhere between twenty-two and twenty-eight thousand a
month depending on acreage and how many holes. And there's a bonus if the course hits the
member satisfaction target, usually another five percent on the year.

The command

Form: /discovery-sheet <transcript> "<prospect>". This run:

/discovery-sheet calls/fairway-fern-2026-03-10.txt "Priya Lindqvist"

Point it at a transcript (.txt, .md, .vtt, .srt, or a recorder's .json) or at your own notes. The prospect name is optional; quote it if it has spaces. Both files land beside the input. Nothing in them comes from outside the file: no web search, no invented mood, no rapport that was not said in words.

What it does

  1. Read the whole file before writing a word. Note the source type, call date and time, duration, who was on it, what was shown and who did most of the talking. Set the quote grade once: VERBATIM for a transcript, NOTES for paraphrase, MIXED for an extract.
  2. Build the fact ledger. One line per fact, quote first, tagged NUM, BINDS, Q, NEXT or ICP. No interpretation yet.
  3. Name the one number. The single figure that decides whether the deal is worth doing and what to charge for it. Captured, it leads THE NUMBERS. Not captured, it becomes line one of the SKELETON.
  4. Write CALL NOTES. Fixed section order. One bold figure per bullet with the quote it came from. Interpretation labelled every time: Arithmetic, Read or Unverified. What was not said is recorded as a fact.
  5. Write the SKELETON. Fact bullets the seller can read aloud, not a script. BRIEFED gives one line to every person, company and document named below it. VERIFY runs before any pitch, show or price.
  6. Review pass. Zero dashes, zero confession words in anything read aloud, every quote findable in the source, every proper noun on the SKELETON in BRIEFED, no invented rapport.
  7. Hand over. The two file paths, the one number and whether it was captured, the WALK OUT WITH list, and any conflicts or speaker-label flags. No re-summary in chat.

What comes out

Two files, written beside the transcript. The first is the record. Its header states where every line came from and the quote grade; THE NUMBERS gives every figure Priya said its own bullet, in bold, followed by the words it came from.

03

one-pager

One personalised page per prospect, built from their own numbers, in your voice, with the three-line email that carries it. Sourced facts only.

The input

A research file on Harbor Lights Hotel, the same fictional hotel that sits in the map above, compiled by Dana Reyes of Fensworth Advisory, with a source beside each fact. Two of its lines:

research/harbor-lights-hotel.md2 of 11 lines
- 64 rooms and suites. Source: harborlightshotel.example home page, fetched 2026-08-18.
- Rate scan: Standard King, 2 adults, 30 nights from 2026-08-20 to 2026-09-18, the hotel's own site against its largest OTA listing ("the OTA" below). Own site higher on 19 nights, equal on 8, lower on 3. Sum of the gaps on the 19 higher nights: $304. Source: research/harbor-lights/rate-scan.csv, collected 2026-08-18 14:00 local.

The second input is Dana's voice profile: her greeting, her exact sign-off ("Best," then "Dana"), her sentence length (median 13 words, longest 24), the words she favours, and the sender line that fills the page footer.

The command

Form: /one-pager <research-file> [voice-profile]. This run:

/one-pager research/harbor-lights-hotel.md voice-profile.md

The research file needs at least three facts with sources (a URL, a document, a filing, or "call with <name>, <date>"). The voice profile is optional; the skill can build one from 5 to 10 of your own unassisted messages. Output goes to one-pager/ beside the research file. The page is the personalisation; the email stays at three lines.

What it does

  1. Build the claim ledger. Every prospect-specific fact as a row: claim, source, date checked, status. VERIFIED and STATED may appear on the page as statements; BENCHMARK once, labelled; INFERRED only as a question or an if; UNSOURCED never.
  2. Put the situation in their own numbers. Up to three figures from the ledger, computed in the notes, in the prospect's own units and wording.
  3. Choose one finding. Specific to this prospect, sourced, consequential in their numbers, and something they can act on. The two runners-up go in the notes with a line on why they lost.
  4. Write what it would take. Three steps at most, concrete enough to start on Monday, and one effort line. No pricing unless the research file supplies it with a source.
  5. Write the ask. One question with a concrete unit of time. Never two options.
  6. Fill the page. Copy the template, replace every placeholder, delete what was not used. The footer carries the sender line and the sourcing standard in one sentence.
  7. Write the email. A subject of six words or fewer, three body lines (what this is, the finding with its one figure, the ask), then the sender's sign-off exactly as the profile gives it.
  8. Self-review, fix, re-run. Grep all three files for dashes and the page body and email for banned phrases, count the words, print to one sheet. Fix every hit and run again until clean; the final output goes in the notes.
  9. Report. The three paths, the finding in one sentence, and the INFERRED and UNSOURCED rows to verify before sending.

What comes out

Three files in one-pager/. The page opens with the situation in the hotel's own numbers, then one finding:

Harbor Lights Hotel: direct bookings over the next 30 nights

Prepared for Marisol Vance, General Manager · 18 August 2026

Where Harbor Lights stands
64rooms and suites, per the hotel's own site
19 of 30nights where the hotel's own site is priced above the OTA (Standard King, 2 adults, 20 Aug to 18 Sep)
3 of 40most recent OTA reviews where the guest says they gave up on the hotel's site and booked on the OTA

Harbor Lights takes bookings on its own site and is listed on the OTA. Peak season runs June to September, so the nights in the scan fall inside the busiest weeks of the year. The scan priced the same room for the same two adults on both channels, night by night, on 18 August.

One finding

On most of the next 30 nights, a guest who prices a Standard King on harborlightshotel.example sees a higher rate than the same room on the OTA. A guest who stays on the site then has to open an account before paying. Recent OTA reviews include guests who say they gave up on the hotel's site and booked on the OTA instead.

A booking that starts on the hotel's site and ends on the OTA carries the OTA's commission. Matching the rate and shortening the booking flow would remove both reasons to leave.

Industry surveys put OTA commission for independent hotels in the region at 15 to 18% (Coastal Lodging Association, 2025 distribution survey).

Below the finding, the page carries What it would take (three steps and one effort line), One ask, and a footer with Dana's sender line and the sourcing standard in one sentence. The $304 across 19 nights becomes $16 a night in the notes and sets the first step: match the own-site rate to the OTA on every night in the scan.

The email carries the page. Line one is the only rapport allowed, a sourced meeting stated as the fact it is. Line two is the finding with the page's one figure. Line three is the ask.

The notes file is the receipt: a claim ledger of 13 rows, each with its source, the date checked and a status. Row 11, a 2024 news item saying the hotel added 12 rooms, is UNSOURCED and stays off the page. Row 9, a phone number that differs between the hotel's site and its business profile, is kept for the call. The last block records the checks:

one-pager/harbor-lights-hotel-notes.mdexcerpt
## Checks run

The reply in chat gives the three paths, the finding in one sentence, and the INFERRED and UNSOURCED rows to check before sending. Nothing else.
